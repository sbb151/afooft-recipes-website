Bouillabaisse

Bouillabaisse is just a fancy name for fish stew, but it is delicious, almost sublime, when made with fresh seafood and vegetables. We first had this in Marseille, maybe the home of the world’s best Bouillabaisse. But now we seem to have this most often during those chilly winter days, especially Fridays during lent.

We typically make this without lobster, mussels, or clams, but you can certainly add these as well.

And, the Rouille is a special treat if you like to spice things up a bit. They say that Bouillabaisse without Rouille is like Marseille without sunshine.

SERVES: 4
ACTIVE TIME: 1 HOUR
START TO FINISH: 1 HOUR

Bouillabaisse

For the croutons, brush the baguette slices with the olive oil and bake in a 250 degree oven until crisp, about 30 minutes. (Or, just toast the slices.)
Rub one side of the toasts with the cut side of the garlic.

FOR CROUTONS
8 or more baguette slices
3 tablespoons olive oil
1 garlic clove, halved

FOR SOUP
2 large tomatoes, peeled,
seeded and coarsely
chopped (could be
canned with juices)
1 onion, chopped
2 garlic cloves, chopped
¼ cup olive oil
1 medium potato, diced

1/3 cup finely chopped fennel
fronds (or 1 teaspoon
fennel seeds)
1 bay leaf
¼ teaspoon crumbled saffron
1 tablespoon sea salt
¼ teaspoon pepper
4 cups white fish stock (or
vegetable stock or water)
1 ½ pounds white fish fillets,
some thin so they almost
dissolve and some thick
12 large shrimp, cleaned
12 large sea scallops

Cook the tomatoes, onion, and garlic in olive oil in a large pot over moderate heat, stirring occasionally, until onion softens, about 7 minutes.
Meanwhile, peel the potatoes and cut into ½-inch cubes.
Stir potatoes into onion-tomato mixture with the fennel fronds, bay leaf, saffron, sea salt, and pepper.

Add the stock and bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer, covered, until the potatoes are almost tender, 8 to 10 minutes.

Cut the fish into bite-sized chunks and add to the soup.
Simmer, covered, about 5 minutes.
Add the shrimp and scallops, and simmer, covered, until they are just cooked through, about 5 minutes.
(If you added some clams or mussels with the shellfish, cook until their shells have opened wide.)
Arrange 2 croutons in each of the soup bowls (I preferred relatively flat bowls.)
Carefully transfer fish and shellfish from the soup to the croutons with a slotted spoon.
Then ladle some broth with the vegetables over the seafood.
Top each serving with a teaspoon of rouille and serve remainder on the side.

FOR ROUILLE
1/8 teaspoon saffron
¼ teaspoon hot water
1 cup mayonnaise
¼ cup roasted red peppers
¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil
½ teaspoon fresh lemon juice
2 teaspoons finely minced
garlic
½ teaspoon cayenne

Sprinkle saffron over hot water and let stand for a minute.
Blend saffron mixture with all the remaining ingredients and ¼ teaspoon salt in a blender until smooth.
