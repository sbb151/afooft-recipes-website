Bolognese

We were so excited the day we saw Grace Pilato’s picture in the CDT. She is a good friend, a first class artist, and great cook. In the article, she featured this Bolognese sauce, which also later appeared in her cookbook, “Cooking With Grace.”

This has become one of our most favorite things, often requested by the girls when we ask what special treat they’d like for dinner.

And, it freezes well, making it great to use another day or as part of a delicious lasagna.

SERVES: 4 (WITH PLENTY EXTRA)
ACTIVE TIME: 45 MIN
START TO FINISH: 2 HOURS

Bolognese Sauce (Ragu)

Heat the butter and olive oil over medium heat in a large skillet. Add the onion, celery, carrot, and bacon to the pan and sauté gently for 10 minutes.
Add the group of meats. Breakup, stir, and sauté for about 7 to 10 minutes until cooked.

At this point, I usually (and carefully) put the meat mixture in a food processor to grind it up a little into smaller, more homo-geneous chunks.

Add the milk and cook on medium-high for 5 to 10 minutes.
Stir in the Parmesan thoroughly.
Pour desired amount over the hot pasta (I usually make penne regate), sprinkle with a little more fresh Parmesan, and garnish with fresh basil.

2 medium onions, finely diced
2 celery stalks, finely diced
2 carrots, finely diced
4 ounces bacon, finely diced
6 ounces butter
2 tablespoons olive oil

2 pounds ground meat, a
combination of beef, pork,
and veal

1 cup dry white wine
¼ teaspoon nutmeg
8 tablespoons tomato paste
4 cups meat broth
1 ½ cups milk
1 cup Parmesan cheese, plus
more for serving

3 sprigs of fresh basil for garnish

Add the wine and allow it to cook down and evaporate, another 5 to 10 minutes.
When the wine has been absorbed by the vegetables and meat, stir in the nutmeg, tomato paste, and broth. Add salt and pepper, according to taste.
Cook the sauce for about an hour (could be just 30 minutes if you are in a big hurry). The mixture will thicken a bit.

Here’s the picture of Grace from the newspaper. She suggests having this with a green salad, crusty Italian bread, and hearty red Italian wine, such as a Valpolicella or a Chianti.
