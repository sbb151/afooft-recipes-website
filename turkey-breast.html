Turkey Breast

Turkey Breast can be so good, but it’s usually way overcooked as part of a special Thanksgiving or Christmas meal, when the whole bird gets roasted. The breasts cook more quickly and then dry out as the dark meat in the legs and thighs cooks to the right temperature.

All kinds of “solutions” regularly appear in the cooking books and magazines, from marinating to brining to injecting, cooking upside down, flipping the turkey repeatedly, roasting in a bag, strategically placing bacon or foil at just the right moment in the cooking process, and on and on.

I think the best way to solve this vexing problem is simply to cook the breast separately. This recipe is a whole lot easier than roasting the entire turkey, and, as an added benefit of the great taste and simplicity, you might get to enjoy this succulent treat more often!

This approach is based on a recipe from Eric Ripert, who, paradox-ically, is the star chef of probably the best seafood restaurant in the world, Le Bernardin, in New York City. His approach also involved doing all kinds of other special things, such as folding cabbage leaves around a spiced confit of thigh meat or making baby stuffed pumpkins with sausage and turkey liver. But the best part of his “French Thanksgiving” menu was how he did the breast (and you’ll know that it’s French because the turkey is cooked in plenty of butter!).

If you’re cutting up a whole turkey, you can roast the legs, thighs, and wings separately or use these dark-meat parts to make a special turkey stock, for gravy or a soup. (Or, make up a recipe for some fancy cabbage pouches, with dark meat!)

Turkey Breast

1 whole turkey breast,
with bone (not backbone),
split down the middle
½ pound butter (two sticks)

Gravy
½ cup unbleached flour
1-2 cups turkey or chicken stock

Preheat oven to 400 degrees.
Place a roasting pan over medium-high heat and add the butter.
Season the breasts on both sides with salt and pepper. When butter is sizzling, add the breasts, skin down. Sear about 5 minutes on each side, or until golden brown.

SERVES: 8
ACTIVE TIME: 15 MIN
START TO FINISH: 1 HOUR

Transfer the pan to the oven and roast breasts for 45 minutes, basting with the butter every 5 minutes. Remove and let rest for 15 minutes.
Meanwhile, pour off (and save in case you need it) about half the butter. Sprinkle the flour around the pan and stir well to incorporate and cook it for a minute or so.
Whisk in the stock a little at a time, until you get a “loose” mixture, also scraping up the bits on the bottom of the pan. Adjust the seasonings, liquid, and extra butter mixture to taste as the sauce thickens to a nice gravy.








When you’re ready to serve, you can cut thin lengthwise slices off the breast as is often done. Or (what I prefer), cut each breast half off the bone, then slice cross-sectionally into “filets” of almost 1-inch thickness.
