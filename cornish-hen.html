Cornish Hen

For some reason, we had never had Cornish hens before reading a late- 1970’s New York Times recipe for them by Gene Hovis, author of the “Uptown Down Home Cookbook.” Now they are one of our favorite poultry dishes. These little chickens are so much fun to eat, whether with a simple roasting or with a tasty little barbecue sauce, as in this recipe.

Usually we prepare one for each person and each of us works on our own bird, with a community bone plate. Unlike regular chickens, all the parts seem to be tender and moist (and succulent and tasty).

I suppose you could eat these with a knife and fork, but they’re much better, and easier, with your fingers – “finger-lickin’ good.”

Cornish Hen

With the Cornish hens breast side down, use some kitchen shears (or a small sharp knife) to cut out the backbone. Then pound the breastbone a little so that the bird lies flat (i.e., “butterflying” it).

SERVES: 4
ACTIVE TIME: 30 MIN
START TO FINISH: 1 HOUR

4 Cornish hens (sometimes
call Rock Cornish hens)
1 stick butter

1 cup onions, finely chopped
¼ cup honey
¼ cup dark brown sugar
¼ cup Worcestershire sauce
1 garlic clove, minced
1 tablespoon dry mustard
1 teaspoon chili powder
1 cup Sauterne or sweet
white wine
¼ cup sherry wine vinegar

Coat the two sides of the hens generously with the sauce.
In a large skillet (or two) heat the butter until foaming. Place the birds skin side down in the hot fat and sauté for about 15 minutes until golden. (This may be done hours ahead of serving time.)








Transfer the Cornish hens to a baking pan skin side up. Bake 20 minutes longer or until tender. Baste with the juices from time to time, adding more sauce if needed.

Combine all the barbecue sauce ingredients in a small saucepan and bring to a boil.
Reduce the heat and simmer for 10 minutes.

You could also just roast the whole Cornish hens with some spices in a 400 degree oven for about 30 minutes. Yum, still finger lickin’!

Preheat the oven to 375 degrees.
