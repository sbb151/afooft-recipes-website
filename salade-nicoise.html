Salade Nicoise

We first had this in the Fall of 1991, after the recipe appeared in The New York Times (from Jacques Pepin). It was so good – we thought of it as the “recipe of the year!”

Somehow, we lost the original instructions. But, when Lauren was working at the PSU library - voilà - she dug it up from old microfiches!

Don’t make this with canned tuna unless you absolutely have to.


SERVES: 4
ACTIVE TIME: 45 MIN
START TO FINISH: 45 MIN

Salade Niçoise

Bring water (with a little salt) to boil in saucepan.
Peel the potatoes, if desired, and cut into small bit-sized pieces.
Boil until tender but still firm, 7-10 minutes. Drain and let cool.
Meanwhile, prepare the dressing. In a little electric mixer (or by hand), mix well together the mustard, garlic, vinegar, olive oil, and 1 teaspoon salt and ½ teaspoon pepper (or to taste).
Add the potatoes to the dressing.
Using the same water or a new pot of salted boiling water, boil the green beans 3 minutes (or less for haricot verts, or more for larger beans). Drain under cold water for a few seconds to stop the cooking and preserve the bright green color. Depending on the size, cut the beans for easy eating.
Peel the yellow (or red or orange) pepper as best you can with a vegetable peeler and cut into thin julienne strips.
Cut the tomatoes in half across the stem, squeeze out the seeds, and cut the unpeeled flesh into 1-inch pieces.
No more than 1 hour before serving, add the beans, pepper, onions, tomatoes, and olives to the potatoes. Mix well.

Cut the tuna into 1 inch cubes.
When almost ready to serve, heat a large heavy skillet over high heat for 4 or 5 minutes, until very hot. Sprinkle the tuna liberally with black pepper and salt and then roll in the canola oil.

Place the tuna cubes in the hot skillet and sauté over high heat for 2-3 minutes until they are lightly browned on the outside and cooked until rare on the inside.
To serve, line a large platter with the lettuce leaves. Mound the Salade Niçoise in the center of the platter and sprinkle the freshly cooked tuna and the basil leaves on top.
Serve immediately.

2 medium baking potatoes

1½ tablespoons Dijon mustard
5 cloves garlic, crushed
1½ tablespoons red wine vinegar
7 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil

¾ pound green beans (haricot
verts if available), cleaned
1 yellow pepper
2 medium tomatoes
1 cup sliced red onions or
scallions
1 cup Niçoise or black olives

1 pound+ good fresh tuna
1 teaspoon canola oil
1 bag interesting lettuce
1 cup (loose) basil leaves,
shredded in large pieces

You don’t need much else with this besides some crusty bread and a nice wine (maybe a tasty Char- donnay). You might even follow up with a little cheese to round out this delicious dish.
